About Us

{{legal_name}} is a community charity in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. We help Jewish families through hard times with practical, discreet support.

Our history

For more than a decade, Israeli Mothers of Crown Heights has quietly assisted families in the Crown Heights community.

It started the way so much good in a close-knit neighborhood starts: a group of Israeli mothers noticed when a family nearby was struggling, and did something about it. A meal for a family with a new baby. Groceries before Shabbos for a household where a parent had lost work. A collection among friends when a neighbor could not cover the rent. Over the years, families learned that there was someone they could turn to, and that their privacy would be respected.

As the need grew, so did the responsibility. Families were coming to us with larger and more urgent needs, and friends and supporters wanted to give in a way that was organized, accountable and tax-deductible.

In 2026, the organization formally incorporated and received IRS 501(c)(3) charitable status to expand its ability to serve the community and provide donors with a transparent, accountable nonprofit organization.

  1. 2010s

    Mothers in Crown Heights begin informally helping families in need: meals, groceries and help with urgent bills.

  2. Over the years

    Word spreads. The network becomes a trusted, discreet resource for families across the neighborhood.

  3. 2026

    Incorporated in New York as Israeli Mothers of Crown Heights Incorporated and recognized by the IRS as a 501(c)(3) public charity.

Who we serve

We focus on Jewish families living in Crown Heights, Brooklyn, and, as our charter allows, members of the broader Jewish community in New York and across the United States. Many of the families we help are large households, young families, single parents, new immigrants and families dealing with illness or a sudden loss of income.

How we work

  • Discretion first. The identities of the families we help are kept confidential. Help is offered quietly, in the spirit of giving without embarrassing the recipient.
  • Neighbors helping neighbors. Our volunteers live in the community. We know the neighborhood, its schools, stores and institutions, and can respond quickly.
  • Practical help. Wherever possible, assistance goes directly toward a specific need, such as a bill, groceries or essentials for children.
  • Accountability. As a registered public charity, we keep records of funds received and assistance given, and our Board of Directors oversees how donations are used.
